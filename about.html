About CleanEarth Community Service Project

Project documentation, technical architecture, community outreach methodology, limitations, and future scope.

Executive Summary

CleanEarth is an undergraduate Community Service Project (CSP) website created to help households, students, and community volunteers in Andhra Pradesh understand and practise waste separation at source.

Rather than claiming hypothetical impact figures, CleanEarth functions as an educational reference tool. It explains the four-bin framework plainly, provides direct lookups for 40+ household items, offers interactive practice tools, and records field survey feedback directly on volunteer devices.

Problem Statement

Municipal solid waste management in urban and peri-urban areas encounters severe operational hurdles when waste is mixed at the source.

When wet kitchen waste, recyclable dry goods, hazardous materials, and electronic scrap are discarded together, organic material spoils recyclable paper and cardboard, broken glass and batteries injure sanitation workers, and compost made from contaminated wet waste is unsafe for agricultural soil. Clear, accessible guidance at the household level is the essential first step.

Key Modules and Architecture

Bin Guide

Detailed instructions on what goes into each bin, what must be kept out, handling practices, and common household examples.

Item Directory

Search through over 40 household waste items to find the right bin and handling tip instantly.

Bin It Right Game

Practice sorting 15 random items into the correct bins with immediate feedback.

Eco Quiz

Test your knowledge on segregation, safety, composting, and recycling rules with 10 questions.

5 R's Hierarchy

Understand waste management priorities from Refuse and Reduce down to Recycling.

Footprint Calculator

Estimate your household's annual waste generation and see the effect of source segregation.

Community Survey

Record waste segregation habits, community barriers, and awareness levels during field visits.

Household Pledge

Commit to simple daily waste segregation actions and generate a printable certificate.

E-Waste Centres

Look up sample recycling plants and collection drop-off points with accepted item filters.

Implementation and Technical Design

The website is built entirely using standard HTML5, CSS3, and vanilla JavaScript. It has no external dependencies, no JS libraries, no backend servers, and no build pipeline. It runs immediately when opening index.html directly from a local drive and deploys without modification to GitHub Pages.

Application Architecture Flow

The browser loads the static HTML structure; CSS variables supply light or dark themes without page flickering; JavaScript modules handle live filtering, quiz logic, input validation, and localStorage operations locally without network roundtrips.

Technical Principles Applied:

  • Zero frameworks: Built solely with native DOM APIs, ES5/ES6 syntax, and standard CSS3 custom properties.
  • Strict flat colour styling: No background gradients, zero emojis, and zero non-standard animations.
  • High accessibility contrast: Maintained strictly above 4.5:1 ratio across light (#F8F7EF) and dark (#10241F) palettes.
  • Privacy-first storage: Surveys, quiz scores, and settings persist exclusively within client-side localStorage.

Field Survey and Community Outreach

During field visits, student volunteers visit households to introduce the four-bin system, demonstrate sorting with the 'Bin It Right' game, answer resident questions using the item directory, and administer the survey.

Evaluation Indicators

  • Number of outreach participants and completed field surveys.
  • Change in correct classification answers before and after the briefing session.
  • Self-reported frequency of household waste segregation.
  • Most commonly misclassified items and unresolved community doubts.

No measured results are reported here. Outcomes will be added only after field records are collected and checked.

Limitations

  • Guidance depends on voluntary resident action at home.
  • Calculator estimates depend on self-reported inputs and assumed factors, not physical measurements.
  • A website alone cannot ensure timely or separate municipal collection in every ward.

Future Scope

  • Expansion of Telugu audio guidance for elder residents and non-literate community members.
  • Ward-specific collection timetable integration with local municipal corporations.
  • Broader physical device testing and offline progressive web application enhancements.

Residents should also check local municipal instructions.
